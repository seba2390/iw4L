# Black Ops — fidelity ledger

Rule: the Black Ops port reproduces the original game, driven by the game's own
data (zones, menus, fonts, materials, sound banks, scripts). No hand-made
substitutes, no Modern Warfare 2 stand-ins, no guessed constants, no stubs that
pretend to work. When the real behaviour cannot be recovered, the item goes to
**Waiting on the maintainer** with what is needed, and work moves on to the next
item.

This ledger lists every shortcut found in the port so far. An item is closed
only when the original behaviour is in place; it then moves to **Closed** with
the commit that fixed it. New shortcuts are not added to the code; if one is
found it is logged here first.

A rule `game_t5` answers as Unknown carries an id (`unknown!("t5.<area>.<rule>",
…)`); its entry names that id in backticks and the trait method it concerns.
`cargo xtask boundary` refuses an id that is not listed here.

Severity: **V** visible, **A** audible, **G** gameplay, **I** invisible.

## Waiting on the maintainer

Items that cannot be closed from the game's data or scripts alone: they need a
reference capture from the original game, or a rule that lives only in the
executable. Agents list them here with exactly what is needed and move on; they
do not work around them.

The CEG-filled `BlackOps.exe` and `BlackOpsMP.exe` are available locally (see
Closed); rules that live only in the executable are worked from them.

The quickest way to answer most of them is a set of screenshots of the original
game at 1280x720 in Kino solo; each entry names the shot it needs.

## Rendering and visuals

- [x] **V** Black Ops vision sets were never loaded, and T5 maps were handed
      Modern Warfare 2's visions. The T5 walks now read every
      `vision/*.vision` raw file with Black Ops' own field table (68 fields:
      film, revive, sound filters, S-curve, bloom, streaks; its types, its
      `unknown`/`malformed dvar` warnings, a field taken once per file, an
      unread field left zero as in a fresh vision slot) and derive the film
      constants as its renderer does: colour temperature (CIE locus, Bradford
      adaptation from 6500 K), hue and saturation matrices snapped to 1/1024,
      times tint and contrast per zone, the bleach row and the zone ranges,
      and the S-curve constants. Kino loads 95 visions; its own:
      `t5 vision: vision/zombie_theater.vision film=true lut=0 s_curve=1`;
      the film and S-curve constants (`game_t5/src/vision.rs`) match an
      independent double-precision computation. T5 maps no longer get Modern
      Warfare 2's visions.
- [x] **V** `t5.render.post_chain`: Black Ops' visions were loaded but nothing
      drew with them; T5 frames ran the renderer's Modern Warfare 2 chain with
      film off. `game_t5::post_chain` now gives the renderer Black Ops' own
      chain as a `PostChainRules` (targets, pass order, every pass's
      constants transcribed from the executable), and the renderer runs it on
      every 3D frame with the map's `code_post_gfx` materials:
      `bloom_downsample_ldr`, `resample_final`,
      `bloom_downsample_convolution`, `bloom_remap`, `bloom_blur_x`/`_y`,
      `bloom_remap_streak` and `bloom_streak_x`/`_y` twice, `bloom_smooth_a`,
      `bloom_apply_ldr` (S-curve) and `postfx_color` (film,
      `postFxControl0..A`) into the frame. Their controls are the renderer's
      post bank, their `TEXTURE_0..3` its post textures. Kino:
      `post chain: READY passes=13 size=UVec2(2560, 1440) […] blend=[]`.
      Black Ops' other passes (`bloom_expansion`, the mip-3 blur,
      `bloom_smooth_b`, the streak apply and the LUT passes) are never chosen
      by the vision state this build reaches, so they are not drawn; controls
      9 and C are only read by `bloom_expansion` and stay zero.
- [ ] **V** `t5.render.post_sampler_state`: Black Ops' post chain sets no
      sampler state for the code textures it binds; they keep the defaults
      its command buffer starts from, which were not found. The chain's
      passes read them linear and clamped (the one state Black Ops itself
      sets, on the first post texture). The scene is read as stored (Black
      Ops sets no sRGB read for it either). *Needs:* the code-image
      sampler-state defaults in Black Ops' command-buffer setup.
- [ ] **V** `t5.vision.channels`: Black Ops picks one of ten vision channels
      each frame (naked, night, flare, infrared, last stand, death, low
      health, water, …) by priority, lerps each per field (bool and lut snap
      at half way, floats and vectors lerp), and copies the winner into the
      film block. Not ported: frames draw with the map's own vision (the one
      its scripts set naked at start), and `visionsetlaststand` stays a
      no-op.
- [ ] **V** Zone data dropped for T5 maps: dynamic entities (Kino's clipmap
      lists 398 client and 142 server ones, Five's 471 and 215:
      `clipmap: dyn ents`; none spawn) and dynamic lights. Effect models are
      read from the common zones (`FX model generation: … retained=105`);
      Kino has no glass.
- [x] **G/V** Static models whose model lives in another zone were neither
      drawn nor solid: 58 of Kino's 3326 slots (36 `zombie_teddybear`, 12
      lobby door tear-ins, perk bottles) are `,name` stubs, and the map walk
      only knew its own models. The map lane now keeps those slots and their
      clipmap rows; the zombie walk builds their meshes in the zone that
      holds them (`common_zombie`, its patch, and the localized
      `en_zombie_theater` for the tear-ins), with materials linked like the
      zombie scene models (`,name` materials bound by name), and their
      collision comes from the same model. Kino: `foreign static models: 58
      placed from 11 meshes (0 surfaces without a material), 49 with
      collision`; Five: 14 placed, 9 with collision.
- [ ] **V** No ragdoll: `startragdoll`/`isragdoll` accepted without effect, dead
      zombies hold their death pose; `launchragdoll` errors on actors.
- [ ] **V** `gib` is a no-op: no thrown body parts.
- [ ] **V** No-ops: `haseyes` (eye glow),
      `settransported`, `vibrate`, `useweaponhidetags`, `setenemymodel`.
- [ ] **V** Recorded but unused: `visionsetlerpratio`, `setplayerrenderoptions`,
      `starttanning`, `setvolfog`, `playfxontagforclients`; `glassradiusdamage` no-op.
- [ ] **V** Not implemented: `setdoublevision`, `setculldist`,
      `start3dcinematic`/`pause3dcinematic`/`stop3dcinematic`/
      `getcinematictimeremaining` (projector),
      `hideviewmodel`/`showviewmodel`, `playweapondeatheffects`.
- [ ] **V** Script hud elem colours: unverified whether Black Ops treats them as
      linear (the round chalk looks darker than the original).
- [ ] **V** `t5.vision.shellshock` (`GameVision::shellshock`): Black Ops'

## Audio

- [ ] **A/G** `PlaySound(alias, notify)` raises its notify after a fixed 2 s
      (`SOUND_DONE_MS` in `natives/engine.rs`) instead of the alias length.
- [ ] **A** `playsound` plays once at the entity's origin and does not follow it;
      so do a weapon event's sounds heard from another player (Black Ops
      plays them on the shooter's entity).
- [ ] **A** Modern Warfare 2's code sounds are not played in Black Ops
      matches: its movement sounds (footsteps, gear rattle), menu code sounds,
      announcer lines and shellshock loops name Modern Warfare 2 aliases, which
      a Black Ops bank does not hold (Kino prepares 1767 missing aliases, was
      110). Black Ops' own rules for those sounds are not known.
- [ ] **A** Footsteps: Black Ops' client hands every footstep to its client
      scripts (a player's footstep event and an AI's `footstep_` notetrack
      call `_footsteps.csc`, which builds `fly_step_<move>_<surface>` and its
      `_plr` / `_npc` form). The local player's footsteps, jumps and landings
      reach them; an AI's `footstep_` notetracks and other players'
      footsteps do not.
- [ ] **A** Not implemented: the server's `stopsound` method, `setsoundblend`.
- [ ] **A** `t5.audio.fire_autosim` (`ModeRules::client_fire`): Black Ops'
      client plays an automatic weapon's shot from a player it does not view
      in first person (and a turret's, and any in a killcam) through its
      automatic fire simulation (`snd_autoSim`, on): the sound is replayed
      every fire time while the shooter's entity carries the firing flag, at
      most three for a three-round burst, and of two such shots of one
      weapon in the same frame less than 30 degrees apart from the view
      (`snd_autosim_window`) only the nearer plays. Those shots play no
      fire sound; single-shot weapons (the M1911) play theirs. Co-op only
      (and killcams). *Needs:* the simulation in the client, and the firing
      flag the movement sets carried to clients on the shooter's entity.
- [ ] **A** Weapon notetrack sound maps: inline `sndnt#` notes play now that
      the viewmodel plays Black Ops' animations (M1911 and Ray Gun reloads).
      Black Ops' client also runs the definition's key-to-alias map (Ray Gun:
      `ray_reload_battery_in` → `sndnt#wpn_ray_reload_battery_in`) on each
      note; that map is not run, and its bare keys reach the sound prefetch
      as alias names (`prefetch catalog gaps`: `ray_reload_*`, `bowie_*`).
